# Supplementary reading and enrichment

These links come from the Moodle module. Use the syllabus and weekly exercises to direct exam preparation; the enrichment items are not a substitute for them.

## Core bibliography

- **Kenneth H. Rosen, Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications, 8th edition (2019).** Exact weekly sections are listed in the syllabus and the problem sheets. The book itself was not downloaded.
- **L. Lovász, J. Pelikán and K. Vesztergombi, Discrete Mathematics: Elementary and Beyond (2003).** Listed in the module's basic bibliography; not downloaded.

Other bibliography listed by Moodle: Grimaldi, Discrete and Combinatorial Mathematics, 5th edition; Graham, Knuth and Patashnik, Concrete Mathematics; Heather Macbeth, [The Mechanics of Proof](https://hrmacbeth.github.io/math2001/index.html).

## Lean and proof formalisation

Week 2 provides [Lean installation guidance](https://lean-lang.org/install/) and points to the [math2001 repository](https://github.com/hrmacbeth/math2001). The original installation instructions are preserved in the Moodle Teaching Outline. No software was installed as part of this resource collection. The presence of those instructions alone does not establish a requirement to submit Lean code in the exam.

## Enrichment on Moodle

The [course home](https://moodle.ucl.ac.uk/course/view.php?id=56448) includes sections featuring Leslie Lamport, Jeremy Avigad's “Mathematics in the Age of AI”, Leonardo de Moura's “Lean's Impact on Mathematics, Programming, and AI”, and “What was Euclid really doing?”. Open the named sections for their embedded videos; the text interface does not expose their original video URLs.

Week 2 also links [OpenAI's mathematics article](https://openai.com/index/sharing-ai-progress-in-mathematics/) and [Scott Aaronson's discussion](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10169). These are contextual reading links from Moodle, not additional exam-scope claims.
