Bioreactor Motor Speed Controller
The motor/sensor assembly is delicate and should be handled with care.
The motor/sensor supply is 
+5 V
, and it must be powered from the 
Arduino 5 V supply
.
Do not 
use an external supply.
Keep the patch lead reasonably short.
The motor rotates at up to 10000 RPM and has 10:1 gearing to give a drive speed of up to 1000 RPM, from a supply voltage of 5 V.
The Hall sensor gives 70 pulses per revolution of the geared-down shaft, so 1167 pulses/s at 1000 RPM.

The speed sensor and motor connecting cable should be reasonably short, to avoid voltage drops and possible noise pickup. A good MOSFET is needed, with >> 1A rating. The flyback diode is essential to protect the MOSFET from the motor back EMF.
The 470 
uF
 capacitor is used for supply decoupling to prevent the motor pulses from interfering with the sensor output.

Some coding hints/suggestions
Your routine needs to measure the speed of the motor (in RPM say) and adjust the voltage applied to the motor, to attempt to maintain the speed at a set value.
The code overleaf contains a function to measure the motor speed (
measspeed
) and display a smoothed version of this (
meanmeassspeed
) to the serial monitor window. It also blinks the internal LED at a rate of once per revolution.
The motor voltage may be adjusted by assigning a value to the 
Vmotor
 variable. The PWM is set up to be 10-bit, so 
Vmotor
 
= 0-1023 relates to a motor voltage of 0-5 V. 
The update/sampling period is regulated to 10 
ms
, which is plenty fast enough. This is done without using the delay() function. In general, you should 
always avoid using the delay() function 
as it pauses the entire loop and can cause a lot of problems.
Your task is to develop code to adjust the motor voltage using feedback of the measured speed so it achieves a desired set speed, of the order of 300-1000 RPM. Make sure to use 
measspeed
 for this rather than the smoothed version of this variable, 
meanmeasspeed
. A simple approach may work well enough, and in later lectures you will be guided through the design of a PI (Proportional Integral) controller.
